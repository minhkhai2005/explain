# Source hierarchy

Work from the top down. Lower tiers may be used to find leads or plain-language framing, but claims should rest on the highest tier available.

| Tier | Source type | Why it ranks here |
|------|-------------|-------------------|
| 1 | Standards and specifications that define the term | They are the definition; wording is normative and reviewed. |
| 2 | The original paper, patent, or report that introduced the term | Shows the intended meaning and the problem it solved. |
| 3 | Established textbooks and handbooks (recognised publishers, multiple editions) | Peer-reviewed synthesis; usually consistent with tier 1–2. |
| 4 | Peer-reviewed review articles and survey papers | Good for current usage and how the meaning has evolved. |
| 5 | Vendor datasheets, application notes, official documentation | Authoritative for that vendor's product; may use the term loosely. |
| 6 | University lecture notes, reputable encyclopedias | Useful for framing; verify against tiers 1–4. |
| 7 | Blogs, forums, Q&A sites, videos | Last resort. Cross-check every claim against two independent higher-tier sources. |

## Where to look, by industry (non-exhaustive)

- **Electronics / semiconductors**: IEEE standards, JEDEC, IEC, manufacturer datasheets and app notes (TI, ADI, Infineon, NXP), Sedra & Smith, Razavi, Horowitz & Hill.
- **Telecommunications / networking**: IETF RFCs, 3GPP TS/TR, ITU-T recommendations, IEEE 802.x.
- **Software / computer science**: language specifications (ECMA, ISO/IEC), W3C/WHATWG, ACM/IEEE papers, the paper that introduced the concept, official project documentation.
- **Mechanical / civil engineering**: ISO, ASTM, ASME, DIN, Eurocodes, Shigley, Hibbeler.
- **Finance / accounting**: IFRS, US GAAP (FASB ASC), Basel Committee (BIS) documents, CFA Institute curriculum, central-bank publications.
- **Medicine / life sciences**: WHO, MeSH definitions, clinical guidelines, PubMed-indexed reviews, standard textbooks (e.g. Harrison's, Guyton & Hall).
- **Law / regulation**: the statute or regulation text itself, official regulator guidance.
- **Chemistry / materials**: IUPAC Gold Book, ASTM, NIST.

## Verification checklist

- Can every reference be opened or located (URL, DOI, ISBN, standard number)?
- Does the cited section actually say what the report claims?
- Are version and year recorded? Standards change; the definition may differ between editions.
- If tier 1–3 sources disagree, is the disagreement explained in the report?
- Is anything stated without a source? Either cite it, mark it as the author's synthesis, or remove it.
